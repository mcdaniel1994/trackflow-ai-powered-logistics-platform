# Al Engineering Final Project Video Guide

## Purpose of the Video
Congratulations! You've reached the final stage of the Al Engineering program.

For your final project, you will create a short 5-minute presentation video showcasing the Al solution you built, the problem it solves, and the engineering decisions behind it.

This video works for you in two ways:
1. **Career Preparation.** Explaining a technical project clearly, on camera, under time constraints is exactly what happens in technical interviews, portfolio reviews, and conversations with employers. This video is a rehearsal for all of them.
2. **Your Portfolio Piece.** When you're done, you'll have a polished, shareable demo of your work - link it on LinkedIn, include it in job applications, or send it directly to recruiters. A recorded 5-minute pitch is a pre-answered interview question.

**How 4Geeks may use your video:** Selected videos (or clips from them) may be featured publicly by 4Geeks on our website, social media, and materials shared with prospective students as project showcases and graduate stories. By submitting your video, you'll be asked to sign a short media release confirming you're okay with this. If you prefer your video stays private to your cohort, just let us know it will not affect your grade or graduation.

## Video Requirements
- **Length:** Approximately 5 minutes
- **Format:** You presenting your project (recorded video), using screen recordings, demos, and visuals wherever they help explain your work

### Recording quality checklist
A few minimums so your video looks and sounds professional:
- Horizontal (landscape) orientation - no vertical video
- Good lighting - face a window or lamp; don't sit with a bright light behind you
- Clean audio a headset or earbud mic is fine; your laptop mic from across the room is not. Record in a quiet space.
- Readable screen recordings - record your demo at full resolution and zoom in on the parts that matter
- Look at the camera during the introduction and the Q&A section at the end (during the demo, it's fine to look at your screen)

## Video Structure

### 1. Introduction
**Open with a Hook (30 seconds)**
Don't start with your name. Start with the problem or the result, in one sentence then introduce yourself.
> "Restaurants in Miami lose about 4 hours a week answering the same WhatsApp questions. I'm Ana, and I built an Al agent that answers them automatically."

Then briefly cover:
- Your name (and background, if relevant)
- Your project name
- The business or process you automated
- A one-line overview of what you built

### 2. The Problem & Opportunity (45 seconds - 1 minute)
Explain the problem your Al solution addresses:
- What challenge or inefficiency did you identify?
- Who experiences this problem?
- Why is solving it valuable?

### 3. Your Al Solution (2 minutes)
This is the core of your presentation. Explain:
- What did you build?
- How does it work?
- What Al technologies, tools, or concepts did you use?
- What makes your solution useful?

Include a demonstration:
- Show the application working
- Walk through the workflow
- Explain the user experience

Remember: focus on communicating the value, not only the technical details. Imagine your viewer is a smart hiring manager who is not an engineer.

### 4. Engineering Decisions & Challenges (45 seconds)
Every real project involves trade-offs and obstacles. Show how you think like an engineer:
- What was the hardest technical problem you hit and how did you solve it?
- What trade-off or design decision did you make, and why? (e.g., "I chose X over Y because...")
- What are you most proud of in how you built this?

*Framing tip:* Talk like an engineer, not a student. Instead of "I learned how embeddings work," say "I had to get retrieval accuracy up, so I tested three embedding models and switched to X." The first sounds like homework; the second sounds like a hiring decision.

### 5. Your 4Geeks Story - Q&A Closing (30-45 seconds)
Close your video by answering the three questions below, speaking directly to the camera. Answer them exactly as written this section may be edited into Q&A-style videos alongside your classmates' answers, so consistency matters.

**Recording rules for this section:**
1. Answer in complete sentences that include the question. Your answer must make sense on its own.
   - X "Probably time, yeah."
   - ☑ "What almost held me back was my full-time job I didn't think I'd have the hours."
2. Pause for 2 full seconds before and after each answer. This gives editors a clean cut point.
3. Look directly at the lens for this whole section.

**The questions (answer all three):**
1. What made you decide to pursue Al Engineering and what almost held you back?
2. Was there a moment, project, or person during the program when things really clicked for you?
3. What would you tell someone who's on the fence about joining 4Geeks?

## Presentation Tips
- ☑ Speak naturally do not read word-for-word
- ☑ Practice out loud at least twice before recording
- ☑ Focus on the story behind your project
- ☑ Explain concepts clearly, even for someone who is not technical
- ☑ Show your personality and passion for what you built
- ☑ It doesn't need to be perfect - it needs to be you, clearly explaining real work

The goal is not just to show what you created, but to demonstrate that you can communicate the value of an Al solution the skill that gets you hired.
